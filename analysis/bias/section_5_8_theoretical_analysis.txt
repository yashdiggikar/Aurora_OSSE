============================================================
Aurora Section 5.8 Spatial Bias Analysis — Theoretical Background
============================================================

SYSTEMATIC BIAS IN ML WEATHER MODELS
--------------------------------------
ML weather models like Aurora are trained to minimize globally-averaged loss
functions. This can lead to spatially structured systematic biases, particularly:

1. REGRESSION TO CLIMATOLOGY (spectral smoothing)
   - At long lead times, ML models tend toward climatological mean states
   - Manifests as T500 biases near ~0 globally but with reduced spatial variance
   - Aurora's attention mechanism may help preserve spatial structure longer
     than simpler CNN-based models like FCN

2. JET STREAM PLACEMENT ERRORS
   - Z500 and U500 biases often cluster near the mid-latitude jet stream (~40°N)
   - Positive Z500 bias north of jet and negative south = poleward-displaced jet
   - Aurora trained on ERA5 1979–2022 may have learned mean jet position

3. POLAR AMPLIFICATION ERRORS
   - Arctic warming trends in ERA5 training data may introduce polar T500 biases
   - Aurora may systematically overestimate polar warming at long lead times

4. TROPICAL BIAS STRUCTURE
   - In tropics, bias patterns often show stationary wave structures
   - These arise from Aurora's inability to perfectly represent ENSO/MJO variability

HOW INSERTION MODIFIES BIAS
----------------------------
When we force Aurora to use ERA5 truth for one variable at each step:
  1. Direct: the inserted variable has zero bias by construction
  2. Indirect: other variables are modified through Aurora's internal dynamics

If Aurora has learned geostrophic balance (Z500 ↔ U,V):
  Z500 insertion → reduces U,V500 bias in midlatitudes (geostrophic projection)
  U,V insertion → reduces Z500 bias in midlatitudes

If Aurora has learned hypsometric relationship (Z500 ↔ T500):
  T500 insertion → reduces Z500 bias globally
  Z500 insertion → reduces T500 bias globally

FAILURE MODES: WHEN INSERTION WORSENS BIAS
-------------------------------------------
Case 1: Inconsistent internal balance
  Aurora's learned T-Z relationship may differ from the true hypsometric equation.
  Inserting T500=ERA5 may force Z500 into an inconsistent state.

Case 2: Geostrophic imbalance in tropics
  Inserting geopotential in tropics creates a geostrophically-consistent wind
  response, but tropical dynamics are ageostrophic. Result: positive Z500
  insertion → incorrect tropical wind anomalies → worse T bias.

Case 3: Error compensation
  Aurora's control bias in two variables may partially cancel each other. Correcting
  one variable removes the cancellation and reveals a larger net bias.

COMPARISON WITH QG THEORY
---------------------------
Quasi-geostrophic theory predicts the following in midlatitudes:
  Z-insertion → U,V improvement:  ΔU = -(1/f) ∂ΔZ/∂y  [direct geostrophic]
  T-insertion → Z improvement:   ΔZ = (R/g) ΔT × Δ(ln p) [hypsometric]
  U,V-insertion → Z improvement:  geostrophic projection

Any deviation from these expected bias-reduction patterns in Aurora indicates
where its learned dynamics diverge from physical balance laws.

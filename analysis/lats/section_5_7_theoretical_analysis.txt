============================================================
Aurora Section 5.7 Latitude-Band Analysis — Theoretical Background
============================================================

WHY LATITUDE BANDS MATTER
--------------------------
Atmospheric dynamics are fundamentally different across latitude regimes:

1. TROPICS (|lat| < 30°)
   - Weak Coriolis parameter (f → 0 at equator)
   - Thermal wind balance breaks down; geostrophic approximation invalid
   - Convective coupling dominates; CAPE and moisture fluxes govern dynamics
   - Expected: T insertion should minimally improve wind fields
   - Expected: Z insertion has weak coupling to T (no hypsometric balance dominance)

2. MIDLATITUDES (30° < |lat| < 60°)
   - Strong Coriolis; quasi-geostrophic dynamics apply
   - Thermal wind balance: ∂U/∂z = -(g/fT) ∂T/∂y
   - Baroclinic instability drives storm tracks (Eady growth rate ~ ∂T/∂y)
   - Expected: Strong T↔U↔Z coupling (all related through QG equations)
   - Expected: Insertion of any QG variable should improve others

3. POLAR (|lat| > 60°)
   - Large f; strong geostrophic constraint
   - Strong vertical coupling through stratosphere–troposphere interaction
   - Sea-ice boundary creates sharp T gradients
   - Expected: T insertion should show strong response in Z (Greenland, etc.)
   - Expected: Long error correlation lengths due to barotropic structure

PHYSICAL COUPLING MECHANISMS IN AURORA
---------------------------------------
Aurora is an attention-based transformer trained on ERA5. Unlike explicit
dynamical models, coupling emerges through learned associations in the training data.

Key expected couplings (from QG theory):
  T → Z: Hypsometric equation: Z ~ ∫T dp; warm air → high thickness → higher Z
  Z → T: Same relationship in reverse; anomalous Z corrects T thickness errors
  U,V → Z: Geostrophic: U = -(1/f) ∂Z/∂y; wind insertion should project onto Z
  Z → U,V: Geostrophic balance; correcting Z should adjust winds

Latitude-dependent test:
  If Aurora has learned true quasi-geostrophic dynamics, these couplings should
  be STRONGER in midlatitudes and weaker in tropics — exactly as in the real
  atmosphere. Deviations from this expectation suggest Aurora has learned
  spurious or climatological rather than dynamically consistent associations.

WHAT FIGURES 5.36–5.38 TEST
-----------------------------
Each figure shows RMSE time series for 4 runs (control + 3 insertions) in 3 bands:

- If insertion run diverges from control ONLY in the mature phase (day 30–60):
  confirms that forcing is activated after day 30 and propagation takes time.
- If insertion run matches control in all bands:
  the inserted variable has no dynamical influence on the response variable.
- If insertion INCREASES RMSE in polar band but DECREASES in midlats:
  reveals a failure mode — Aurora's polar dynamics are inconsistent with
  theoretical expectations.

COMPARISON WITH FOURCASTNET
-----------------------------
FourCastNet showed [results from FCN analysis in /FourCastNet/lats/]:
  - T-insertion improved Z500 strongly in midlatitudes
  - UV-insertion showed weaker coupling to T500 in tropics
  - Z-insertion dominated geostrophic balance in all bands

Aurora is expected to show similar band structure if its transformer architecture
has learned similar balances. Differences in coupling efficiency between Aurora
and FCN at the same latitude band indicate architectural or training differences.
